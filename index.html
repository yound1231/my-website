Bioinformatics for Data Science Students

Introduction

A beginner’s guide for undergraduate data science students who are curious about applying their skills to biology and medicine. Bioinformatics brings together data science, computing, and life sciences to help make sense of biological data.

What is bioinformatics?

Bioinformatics is the field of developing and using computational methods to analyze, interpret, and store biological data. It helps researchers work with large and complex datasets—from DNA sequences to medical images—to answer biological questions and support advances in medicine.

Application Areas

🧬 Genomics

Description: Genomics is the study of an organism’s complete set of DNA. Bioinformatics helps process and compare genetic sequences to understand how genes work and how variations relate to traits or conditions.

Example of a data science method used: Statistical sequence analysis (such as comparing sequences to identify differences or conserved regions).

🩻 Medical Imaging

Description: Medical imaging techniques like X-rays, CT scans, and MRIs produce large volumes of image data. Bioinformatics and computational approaches help extract meaningful information from these images to support diagnosis and research.

Example of a data science method used: Image processing and pattern recognition (techniques for identifying features or structures in medical images).

💊 Drug Discovery

Description: Drug discovery is the process of identifying and developing new medicines. Computational approaches can help screen potential compounds and predict how they might interact with biological targets, narrowing down candidates for further study.

Example of a data science method used: Predictive modeling (using known data to estimate properties like binding affinity between compounds and targets).

How to get started

Skills to learn

  • Python: A versatile programming language widely used for data analysis and working with biological datasets.
  • Statistics: Essential for interpreting data, testing hypotheses, and understanding variation in biological measurements.
  • Machine learning: Useful for building models that can find patterns in complex biological or clinical data.

Types of public datasets to explore

  • Genomic sequence data: Publicly available DNA/RNA sequence datasets from databases like NCBI (National Center for Biotechnology Information).
  • Medical imaging datasets: Open-access collections of medical images shared for research and education.
  • Compound and bioactivity data: Public datasets of chemical compounds and their biological activity that can be used to explore drug discovery questions.
